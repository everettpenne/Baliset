# Baliset 0.4.0

Albums stay together, loudness evens out, and tracks can meet each other at the edges.

## Albums with their guests

The album view used to split an album whenever the track credit changed — six *Isolation* tracks became six albums ("Kali Uchis",
"Kali Uchis Feat BIA", "Kali Uchis Feat. Tyler, the Creator"...). Now:

- a folder scan treats a folder + album title as one album, whatever the individual track credits;
- an album-artist tag (`TPE2`) wins over the track credit, and when it is absent the main artist is used ("Kali Uchis Feat. X" →
  "Kali Uchis") for the album and artist pages;
- disc numbers (`TPOS`) are read and the album is sorted disc → track, with **Disc 1 / Disc 2** headers and real track numbers;
- a "Various Artists" album is shown as a compilation and its artist line no longer links to a non-existent single artist.

The folder scan now also records album artist, disc and genre, and Android's media index supplies the same fields when it has them.
An album split across separate folders (one folder per disc) is still separate; a single folder with disc tags is one album.

## Volume leveling

A measured target of **−18 dBFS RMS**, a **−0.5 dBFS peak ceiling**, and a ±12 dB clamp:

- ReplayGain (`REPLAYGAIN_TRACK_GAIN`/`_PEAK`) is used when the file carries it;
- 16-bit AIFF PCM is measured directly (no decoder);
- MP3 and other compressed files are decoded once with the platform codec and measured the same way;
- **Settings → Playback → Measure volume** shows progress and can be stopped; the result is one gain per track in `levels.json`;
- the gain is applied in a small audio processor before the sink, for 16-bit and float PCM, with clipping protection; audio-focus
  ducking is a separate factor so it cannot overwrite the leveling gain.

The switch is live: turning leveling off restores unity gain without rebuilding the queue. Nothing is analysed in the background and
nothing leaves the phone.

## Gapless and crossfade

Gapless playback is the original path: leave crossfade **Off** and the single ExoPlayer plays consecutive files with
`setPauseAtEndOfMediaItems(false)` and the same audio sink.

Choose **3, 5, 8 or 12 seconds** and a second ExoPlayer starts the next queue entry at volume 0 while the current one is still
playing. The two volumes ramp in opposite directions, then the media session switches to the new player, so the notification, lock
screen, island and the app all show the new track. Audio focus is requested and released once by the service (not by each player),
and loss/duck is handled there; otherwise the two players would fight over focus.

## What was tried

On the emulator (1080 × 2400, Android 17 / API 37):

- six guest-credited *Isolation* tracks in one folder → **one album, "Kali Uchis"**, in the album grid and album page;
- a hand-built two-disc album → **Disc 1 / Disc 2** headers and track numbers;
- **Measure volume** measured nine tracks in one pass: two AIFFs and seven MP3s, with sensible gains (+0.5 dB, −2.1 dB, −9.1 dB,
  +3.5 dB);
- a **5-second crossfade** from The Cure's 3:33 AIFF into the MP3 moved the session from item 7 to item 8, updated the Now Playing
  page to "Party Rock Anthem", and pause, resume and next all worked afterwards;
- crossfade **Off** played the same queue with no overlap;
- **58 JVM tests** pass, including the ID3/AIFF readers, album grouping/discs, loudness math and the gain processor.

## Limits

- Crossfade, its second player and the manual audio focus were exercised on the emulator, **not on a physical phone**, and not against
  a real phone call or headphone unplug.
- Leveling measures 16-bit AIFF directly; 24-bit/float AIFF plays but is not measured. Compressed measurement uses the platform
  decoder, so an unusual codec the phone cannot decode is skipped rather than guessed.
- Measuring a 33 GB library reads it once and takes minutes; it is deliberately a button, with progress and Stop.
- An album split across separate folders (one folder per disc) is still two albums.

## Installing

Sideload the APK. It is signed with the same key as 0.3.0, so it installs over it and keeps the library, playlists, settings and
saved loudness measurements.

After updating, tap **Settings → Rescan now** once: the album regrouping is stored in the library snapshot, so the old snapshot keeps
the old per-artist albums until it is refreshed.
